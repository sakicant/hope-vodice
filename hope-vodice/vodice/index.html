The town

Vodice

A fishing village that turned into a marina town without losing the fishing. Twelve kilometres from Šibenik, on the Dalmatian coast, and our home.

In short

Small enough to walk, big enough to have everything

Vodice sits in the Šibenik-Knin county on the Adriatic, twelve kilometres north-west of Šibenik. It was a fishing village; it is now a marina and a tourist town, and in August it is busy. In June and September it is not, which is when we would tell a friend to come.

The old walls and a three-storey tower from the sixteenth century are still standing in the centre. The church of Gospa od Karmela sits above the town with its chapels running up Okit hill, which is a short, steep and worthwhile walk for the view back down over the coast.

What to do

Days that work from here

The beach

Mala Vrulje is the closest, 600 metres away, and it is where most of our guests end up. The long pebble stretches towards Amadria Park are a few minutes further and have more shade.

Boats and diving

ACI Marina is 300 metres from the door. Day boats leave for the Kornati islands and for Zlarin and Prvić. Local diving schools run trips out to the reefs and wrecks along this coast.

Krka waterfalls

Twenty-five kilometres away, through Skradin. Go early, before the coaches, and you get the falls to yourself for an hour. It is an easy half day from Vodice.

Eating

Where we send people

Vodice konobas do the Dalmatian things properly: grilled fish, octopus under the bell, black risotto. These three are the ones we name when guests ask.

Getting here

Arriving in Vodice

By plane

Split (70 km) and Zadar (76 km) are both about an hour away by car. Both have car hire at the terminal, and there are shuttle buses to Šibenik.

By car

The A1 motorway runs past Šibenik; come off there and it is twelve kilometres to Vodice. Tell us when you book and we will explain where to leave the car.

By bus or train

There is no train to Vodice. The nearest station is Šibenik, twelve kilometres away, with regular buses on to the town.

Come in June or September

The sea is warm, the konobas have tables, and the town belongs to the people who live in it again.
